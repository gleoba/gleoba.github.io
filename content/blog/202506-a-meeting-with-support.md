Last week, I joined what I thought would be a routine Zoom call with a European vendor. As we waited for everyone to connect, I expected the usual small talk about weather or weekend plans. Instead, 𝙨𝙤𝙢𝙚𝙩𝙝𝙞𝙣𝙜 𝙧𝙚𝙢𝙖𝙧𝙠𝙖𝙗𝙡𝙚 𝙝𝙖𝙥𝙥𝙚𝙣𝙚𝙙.

The meeting leader began not with business updates, but with words of solidarity. He checked in on our well-being, acknowledged the challenges we're facing here in Israel, and expressed genuine empathy for our situation. He spoke about how admirable it is that we continue to maintain business relationships and move forward despite the ongoing threats and disruptions to our daily lives.

I wasn't prepared for this moment of human connection. In a time when missile alerts interrupt our sleep, when we're constantly thinking about the safety of our children and elderly family members, when work meetings are rescheduled around shelter protocols - to receive such unexpected support from a business partner across the world was deeply moving.

Living through this conflict means our "normal" has been redefined. School routines are disrupted, family gatherings postponed, recreational activities put on hold. We've learned to pivot quickly when alerts sound, to comfort scared children while managing our own stress, and to find strength we didn't know we had.

But moments like that meeting remind me of something powerful: human solidarity transcends borders, business relationships can become bridges of support, and sometimes the most meaningful connections happen when we least expect them.

We accomplished our business objectives that day, but what I'll remember most is that opening moment of genuine care from someone thousands of miles away. It reminded me that even in our darkest hours, we're not alone.

What I learned: Business is ultimately about people, and people can surprise you with their capacity for empathy and support when it matters most.

✨ If you've been following the situation here in Israel, your thoughts and support mean more than you know. And if you're going through your own challenges wherever you are in the world, please know that there are people who care – sometimes in the most unexpected places.
